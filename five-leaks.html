The framework

Five ways a census loses money.

Every multi-site network I have opened leaks the same five ways. You can check four of them yourself this week, with one facility and one month.

First principles

Three records that have to agree.

A service is delivered. It is documented. It is billed. Revenue is correct only when all three say the same thing.

Delivered The attendance log Kept at the facility. Often on paper.
=
Documented The signed chart In the EMR, written after the session.
=
Billed The claim Built downstream, off a spreadsheet.
All three agreeRevenue is correct. There is nothing to find.
Any one driftsMoney leaves, and no alarm goes off.

Nothing in a manual process forces them to agree. One person compares a daily log against a chart against a spreadsheet, hundreds of rows, once a week, while doing four other jobs. The five leaks are not a list of mistakes. They are the five ways those three records come apart.

The five

Each leak, and how to catch it.

One service, from the room it happened in to the money arriving. Each leak branches off at the exact point it happens.

How to check each one

One facility, one completed month. No systems access, no vendor, no permission needed. Numbers and colours match the diagram above.

05

Identity mismatches

Do this one first

Pull Your facility attendance log template. Look for a medical record number column.

Look for No MRN column means your team is linking services to billing episodes from memory, every week. Adding the column closes the entire class of error, permanently, for free. At the network in the case study it was the root cause behind every identity mismatch we found.

Thirty seconds. The highest return on this page
01

Unbilled attendance

Pull Attendance marks from one facility's logs for a completed month, and billed entries from the census for the same period.

Look for Two counts that do not reconcile. Every gap is a service you delivered and never charged anyone for.

About an hour
02

Billing above documentation

Pull Twenty billed lines at random. Trace each one back to a signed note.

Look for Lines that resist. More than one or two and it is a process gap, not a documentation gap.

Half a day
03

Threshold days

Pull Last month sorted by hours delivered. Read the bottom of the list, then read the names on it.

Look for Short days clustering on the same clients. That is an engagement conversation, not a billing one.

Twenty minutes
04

Level-of-care conflicts

Pull Every client whose level of care changed. Check the effective date in all three systems.

Look for A date that does not match, or a step-up no authorization ever covered. This one finds real money on the first pass.

An hour or two

Why the error rate is not the point

Every leak above is findable. What decides the money is when you find it.

Day 0 to 7Correction window
Day 7 to 30Narrowing
Day 30 to 90Write-off
Found by a weekly checkThe note can still be written, the authorization still chased, the claim still corrected before it goes out. It costs an hour of someone's time.
Found on a billing company reportThe same error, the same size, ninety days older. There is nothing left to do but write it off.

The error rate barely matters. The correction window is the whole game. That is the argument for running these checks weekly, and upstream of your billing company, and it is the only argument that matters.

The honest boundary

A person can find these. A person cannot find all of them, every week, forever.

Give someone this page, one facility and one completed month, and they will learn more in a day than in a quarter of dashboards. What nobody can do by hand is run it across every facility, every week, before the billing company opens the sheet. That is the only version that lands inside the correction window, and it is the only reason the engine exists.
