AI Chief of Staff Hiring Kit

Hire the IC or the DRI. Never the legacy manager.

20 hr/week. OnlineJobs.ph. The wrong hire here will spend 3 months building dashboards no one looks at. The right hire will close one loop in week one. These questions tell you which one you have in front of you.

The Framework

Three archetypes. Pick one. Reject the third.

Per Jack Dorsey via YC: post-AI companies have IC builder-operators, DRIs, and AI founders. The classic manager middle layer is dead. You are the AI founder. You're hiring for one of the other two.

Hire if

IC / Builder-Operator

ships things weekly

Comes to meetings with working prototypes, not pitch decks. Bias to ship. Lives inside Claude, n8n, Apollo, Notion. Will build the agent stack with you.

Hire if

DRI

owns one number

Strategy and customer outcomes. One person, one outcome, no hiding. Will own a specific KPI like trip-wire conversion rate or cohort fill rate.

Reject

The Manager

routes information

Wants to "build a team," "set up processes," and write status reports. The exact role AI replaces. Expensive human middleware. Do not hire.

IC

Five questions to detect the builder-operator

You're listening for: artifacts, recency, ownership of the build, comfort with tools.

01
Walk me through the last thing you shipped end to end. Not what you wrote a spec for. What you actually built that someone is using today.
Green flagSpecific artifact, named tool, named user, recent (last 30 days). They open a tab and show you.
Red flag"I led a team that..." or "We were responsible for..." or anything older than 90 days.
02
Show me one tool you've built for yourself in the last 6 months. A script, a workflow, an n8n flow, a Claude project. Anything you made because you didn't want to do something twice.
Green flagPulls up a Notion, a GitHub repo, a Make scenario, or a Claude project on the spot. Even ugly is fine.
Red flag"I haven't really had time to build for myself" or pivots to a tool they used.
03
If I gave you Claude API access and a $300/mo budget tomorrow, what would you build for me in week one?
Green flagNames a specific loop in my business and a concrete first artifact. Asks clarifying questions about my data sources.
Red flagGeneric answer like "I'd start with a customer dashboard" or "I'd assess the current state."
04
When you have a problem to solve, do you start by writing a doc, building a prototype, or talking to a person? Pick one.
Green flag"Prototype." Maybe "talking to a person, then prototype." Names friction with doc-first cultures.
Red flag"Doc." Or worse, a long answer about how it depends.
05
What's the highest API bill you've ever knowingly run on a personal project? What did you build for it?
Green flagSpecific number, even if small. Token-comfort. They've felt the meter run.
Red flagHas never paid for an API. Has only used free tiers. Cannot conceptualize a token-max world.
DRI

Five questions to detect the directly responsible individual

You're listening for: ownership of a number, comfort with no hiding, customer obsession, decision speed.

06
Tell me about an outcome you owned where there was nowhere to hide. What was the number, by how much did you move it, over what window?
Green flagSpecific KPI, specific delta, specific timeframe. Owns both the win and the messy parts.
Red flagDiffuse answer about team outcomes. Cannot point to a number that was theirs alone.
07
If I gave you "Brand OS Agent revenue per month" as your one outcome, what's your week-one plan?
Green flagAsks what current revenue is. Names two or three levers (top of funnel, conversion rate, AOV) and which they'd pull first. Concrete.
Red flag"I'd start by understanding the business." Or "I'd build a strategy doc."
08
Something you owned failed. The first hour after you knew, what did you do?
Green flagNames the thing, owns it, explains the action they took, what they changed in the system after.
Red flagBlames a teammate, a tool, the market, or "alignment issues."
09
How would you decide between fixing the trip-wire conversion rate vs. fixing the cohort fill rate, if you could only ship one this week?
Green flagAsks for the unit economics. Reasons about which has more leverage. Has a tiebreaker.
Red flagWants to do both. Cannot reason about leverage. Picks one without explaining why.
10
When you don't have the data you need to make a call, what do you do?
Green flag"I make the call with what I have, write down the assumption, and wire instrumentation so I'm not blind next time."
Red flag"I escalate" or "I wait until we have better data" or "I call a meeting."
FC

Four forced-choice questions

No long answers. They have to pick. The pick reveals their wiring.

11
Working prototype on Friday, OR a customer signing on Friday. Which energizes you more?
ReadPrototype answer = IC. Customer answer = DRI. Both are valid. "Both" is a manager.
12
Build the thing yourself, OR own the outcome it drives. Which do you want?
ReadBuild = IC. Own = DRI. "Manage the team that builds it" = reject.
13
Async by default with weekly sync, OR daily standup. Which fits how you work?
ReadAsync = good. Daily standup = a tell that they want structure to hide inside. Both archetypes here are async.
14
If your work output became fully visible to me in real time, would that feel safe or uncomfortable?
Read"Safe, that's how I already work" = strong yes for either archetype. "Uncomfortable" = they need permission to be invisible. Reject.
Hard Disqualifiers

If you see any of these, end the call early.

Doesn't matter how charming, how well-credentialed, how good their references are. These tells correlate with high-cost, low-output hires every single time in a post-AI company.

✕

Their last meaningful output was a slide deck or a strategy doc, not a thing in production.

✕

They describe themselves as a "manager" or "leader" without showing you a single artifact they personally built.

✕

They want to "build a team" before building anything themselves.

✕

They cannot show you one thing they made in the last 90 days.

✕

They cannot articulate a specific number they personally moved.

✕

They use "we" for everything good and "they" for everything that went wrong.

✕

They've never paid for an API or run an LLM pipeline at any scale.

✕

They get visibly uncomfortable when you describe real-time visibility into their work.

5-Day Paid Trial

Don't hire on interview alone. Ship one loop together.

Pay them for 10 hours of work spread across 5 days. The brief is one closed loop on a real piece of your business. This single test will tell you more than five interviews.

The Trial Brief

10 hours / 5 days / paid at full rate / real production work
Day 1 - 2

Instrument

Pick the trip-wire conversion loop. Wire Stripe + Supabase + a Notion mirror so every $7 buyer gets a row with timestamp and source.

Day 3 - 4

Build the dashboard

Single HTML page or Notion view that shows: $7 buyers this week, % converting to $2K cohort, time-to-conversion, top source.

Day 5

Ship one improvement

Based on data, ship one change to the trip-wire page or the post-purchase email. Document the hypothesis. Wait for next week's data.

What you're testing: Did they ship in 5 days? Did they ask sharp questions? Did the artifact work? Did they propose the next loop without being asked? If yes to all four, hire.

What You Need Before The Call

Bring these four things to every interview.

If you walk in without these, you'll default to vibes. Vibes hire managers.

01

One specific number

The KPI you'd hand the DRI on day one. For Sunny: "trip-wire conversion rate to $2K cohort" or "weekly Brand OS sessions booked."

02

One specific artifact

The thing you'd ask the IC to build first. For Sunny: "instrument the $7 trip-wire and ship a closed-loop dashboard."

03

A 5-day paid trial budget

~$200 to $400. Cheap insurance against a 90-day mis-hire. Tell them upfront the trial is paid and real.

04

The scorecard above, printed

Score during the call, not after. After, your gut will rewrite the score. Don't let it.
