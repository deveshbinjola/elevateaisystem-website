Why the numbers are wrong

Money leaves a manual census five ways.

Not fraud, not carelessness. Attendance lives in one system, Kipu, Sunwave, or a paper log. Money lives in another, the census workbook your billing company reads. A person carries it across by hand every week.

01Unbilled attendanceDelivered, documented, never entered. No claim was ever made, so nothing downstream ever asks.
02Billing above documentationA line the chart cannot support. It bills cleanly today and returns later as a clawback.
03Threshold daysAn IOP day under three hours, a PHP day under five. Reimburses at roughly a tenth, cost you full price.
04Level-of-care conflictsA step-up recorded in one system, with an authorization that never caught up. Denied ninety days later.
05Identity mismatchesMatched by name instead of record number, or a client discharged last month. Hardest to defend afterward.
And why you cannot see itYou find out at denial time, ninety days late, from someone else's report. Or worse: a payer audit finds it 12 to 24 months later, and the recoupment letter asks for money you already spent. Same week is a correction. Ninety days is a write-off. Two years is a takeback.

Questions

What operators ask first.

We already have a billing company.

Keep them. They bill what you send. This makes sure what you send is right, and at the network above, the billing company liked it, because their input got cleaner.

We already run Kipu, or Kipu RCM, or CMD.

Good. Keep those too. Between attendance in your EMR and the file your biller reads, a person still carries the week across by hand. This checks that week before it leaves the building. Nothing gets replaced.

Are you another AI note vendor?

No. We do not write clinical notes and we do not touch claims. The engine drafts the census from attendance you already recorded, runs five checks, and holds the ugly lines for a person to decide.

Our data is too sensitive for AI.

Agreed, which is why patient data never leaves your infrastructure. Local scripts, BAA signed, aggregates only. Ask any vendor where the data physically goes. My answer is nowhere.

We are too messy, and every site does it differently.

Messy is the qualification, not the disqualification. If your spreadsheets were clean you would not need me. Each site has its own dialect of colours, time slots and naming, and the parser learns all of them before anyone talks about standardising.

How do we know it works?

Parallel run. Your person does one normal week by hand, the system does the same week, and we compare line by line before you rely on it.

What if our billing person leaves?

That is the real risk today, and a reason to start rather than wait. The rules come out of their head into config, plus an SOP we write together. The system runs the rules and a human confirms the edge cases.
