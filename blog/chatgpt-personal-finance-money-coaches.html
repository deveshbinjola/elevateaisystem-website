ChatGPT Just Got Inside Your Client's Bank Account. Money Coaches Have a Choice This Week.

The Signal: On May 15, OpenAI launched a Personal Finance experience in ChatGPT for Pro users in the US. Plaid integration. 12,000+ financial institutions including Chase, Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, AmEx, Capital One. A live dashboard of spending, portfolio performance, subscriptions, and upcoming payments. Intuit integration is coming. Plus tier next. Your client now has a 24/7 financial copilot that knows their actual numbers. The "what should I do with my money" coaching call just got commoditized. The work that survives is the work AI cannot do.

I'm going to walk you into the scene before I walk you into the news.

You're on a Tuesday call with a client.

They sit down and say... "I want to talk about my money this week."

Six months ago, that sentence opened a 45-minute conversation about their numbers, their categories, their savings rate, where they should put their next $5K, what to do with the new bonus, whether to refinance.

You earned the call because you held the spreadsheet.

Now read what OpenAI just shipped on May 15.

Your client's ChatGPT is connected to their bank.

Their portfolio.

Their credit cards.

Their subscriptions.

Their upcoming payments.

All twelve thousand of their possible financial institutions.

And it answered the spreadsheet half of that 45-minute call before they got on Zoom with you.

The flow is simple.

Your client opens ChatGPT.

Clicks Finances in the sidebar.

Plaid handles the bank handshake.

A dashboard appears with balances, spending, subscriptions, portfolio, upcoming payments.

Then they type. "Where did my money go this month?"

And the AI doesn't guess.

It answers with their actual transactions.

This is not a budgeting app feature.

This is the financial layer of personal life being absorbed by a chat box.

What This Kills, What It Doesn't

Look at the work you do when a client says "let's talk about my money."

Some of it is now table-stakes commodity.

Some of it just got more valuable than it was last week.

The split looks like this.

01
Reading the spending
"You spent $4,200 on dining last quarter, mostly Friday nights." A live dashboard handles this faster than you can pull up the screenshot.
Exposed
02
Categorizing and prioritizing expenses
"Your top three spend categories and the ones you can cut." Auto-categorization with full transaction history is now a sidebar away.
Exposed
03
Optimizing the math
"Pay down debt at 9% before contributing more to the index fund." This was never a moat. Now it's not even a search.
Exposed
04
Naming what they actually feel about money
The shame around the credit card statement. The freeze around the savings target. The way their breath shifts when they say a real number out loud.
Safe
05
The identity work under the number
"Why does charging $5K feel like asking for too much?" "Whose voice is in your head when you look at the savings line?" No dashboard reaches this floor.
Moat
06
Embodied behavior change
The 90-day pattern of actually sending the invoice, actually raising the price, actually holding the no. AI can suggest. Only a coach can hold the field where the change happens in the body.
Moat

Look at the layers tagged "Exposed."

If 60% of your money coaching is doing those three things, you are now competing with a free dashboard.

Look at the layers tagged "Moat."

If those are the floors you spend most of your call on, your work just got more valuable, not less.

Why This Hits Harder Than The Last Wave

The last AI wave hit content coaches and ad coaches first.

This one hits money coaches, financial planners, fractional CFOs, and any business coach whose call is mostly "let's look at the numbers."

The reason is not the model.

It is the data.

Until now, AI could talk about money in the abstract. Now it can talk about your client's money, with their statements open, in real time. The advice goes from generic to personal in a single connection step.

The "I know your specific situation" angle was your edge.

That edge is no longer exclusive.

What The Old Money-Coach Sales Page Looked Like

What Was On The Old Sales Page

  • "I'll show you exactly where your money is leaking"
  • "We'll build your monthly budget together"
  • "You'll know your numbers cold by month 2"
  • "Personalized financial dashboard, custom-built for you"
  • "Templates, calculators, savings trackers"
  • "30-minute monthly money date with me"

What Needs To Be There Now

  • "I'll show you what your nervous system does at the number"
  • "We'll change the story under the budget, not the spreadsheet"
  • "You'll feel different walking into your bank account"
  • "Identity work under the wealth story you inherited"
  • "Embodied practice for charging, holding, receiving"
  • "Held space when AI gives you the answer and you still can't do it"

The left column got commoditized.

The right column is what coaching always was, underneath the spreadsheet layer that the industry confused for the work.

The Inner Work Version Of This Story

The reason this announcement scares some coaches is not because their job is gone.

It's because the part of the job that felt safe just disappeared.

The spreadsheet was safe.

The worksheet was safe.

The "here's a framework" PDF was safe.

You could hide behind those when the deeper conversation got too close.

The body work was always the work.

It just had a paper trail of templates wrapped around it.

The templates got eaten.

The work didn't.

If the only thing you offered was a spreadsheet review and a budgeting framework, AI just ate your offer. If you offered embodied change around money, AI just made your offer scarce. The same announcement is two different futures depending on where you actually do the work.

What A Money Coach Does This Week

Three moves. None of them are scrambles.

One. Open your last 10 client calls in your memory. Mark each one with what you actually held. Was it the spreadsheet? Or was it the moment they said the real number and their breath caught? Be honest. That ratio is your future.

Two. Rewrite the sales page on your offer. Strip out the budgeting framework promises. Replace them with the deeper layer your client actually paid for. Read the right column above for the syntax.

Three. Test the tool yourself. Connect ChatGPT to one of your accounts. Watch what it does, what it misses, and what it can't reach. Your future positioning is in the things it cannot say to a person who is shaking when they read the statement.

Why The Window Closes Fast

Right now, your clients still default to opening Zoom with you when they want to "look at the numbers."

In ninety days, half of them will have already opened ChatGPT first.

By the end of the year, the spreadsheet layer of the call is solved before they hit your booking link.

You don't have to compete with that layer.

You have to stop selling it.

The coaches who keep the dashboard part as the headline of their offer will be selling a worksheet against a free dashboard.

The coaches who put the body part as the headline will be selling something nothing else on the market can deliver.

Your Move

Pull up your offer doc.

Read the first three bullets out loud.

If the first three bullets describe the kind of work an AI dashboard can now do, you have a positioning problem.

Not a tool problem.

Not a marketing problem.

A positioning problem.

The work you were really doing was always under the spreadsheet.

This week is the week you say that out loud on your sales page.
