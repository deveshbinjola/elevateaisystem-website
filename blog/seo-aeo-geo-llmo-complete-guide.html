How Has Search Fractured Into 4 Channels?

In 2024, SEO meant one thing: ranking on Google's blue links. A company invested in backlinks, keywords, and technical optimization, and if they ranked #1, they got traffic.

That world is gone.

Search has fractured. Today, when someone searches, they might find:

  • Traditional SEO results: Google's blue link rankings (losing importance)
  • AI Overviews: Google's AI-generated summaries that appear above the fold (growing daily)
  • Featured Snippets & PAA boxes: Direct answers in boxes (becoming more common)
  • LLM answers: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity deciding what to recommend (new, rapidly growing)

Most businesses are optimizing for 1 of these 4 channels. The smartest are optimizing for all 4, which means they're getting 2-3x more visibility than their competitors.

This guide is your blueprint for the 4-pillar approach: SEO, AEO, GEO, and LLMO. By the end, you'll know exactly what to do, when to do it, and which tactics move the needle.

What Is SEO?

What It Is

SEO is optimizing your website to rank on Google's traditional blue link results. It's still the foundation of search visibility, but it's not sufficient on its own.

How It Still Works in 2026

Google still ranks websites based on:

  • Relevance: Does your content match the search query?
  • Authority: Do authoritative sites link to you?
  • Experience: Does your content show real experience with the topic?
  • Technical health: Is your site fast, mobile-friendly, and crawlable?

The key change in 2025-2026 is that Google has deprioritized pure keyword matching. It now heavily weights E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authority, Trustworthiness) and original insights.

Google's algorithm has shifted from "Does this page have the keyword?" to "Does this page show real expertise?" This means thin content, AI-generated listicles, and keyword-stuffed pages are dead.

5 Must-Do SEO Tactics for 2026

1. Build Topic Clusters with Internal Linking

Instead of writing 50 standalone blog posts, create 5 pillar pages (comprehensive, 3,000+ words) and 10 child pages (supporting content, 1,500+ words each) that link back to the pillar. Google ranks clusters higher than scattered articles. For example: Pillar on "Coaching Business Fundamentals" with child pages on "How to Set Coaching Rates," "Building a Coaching Niche," etc.

2. Create Original Research or Case Studies

Google's algorithm now heavily favors original data. If you can publish "The 2026 Coaching Industry Report" based on your own data (surveys, interviews, case studies), Google will rank that page higher than a 10th rehashing of industry information. Case studies showing your actual work are gold.

3. Demonstrate E-E-A-T Through About Pages and Author Bios

Have a detailed author bio on every post showing your credentials, years of experience, and achievements. Include an About page showing your expertise, media mentions, and social proof. Link to your LinkedIn, podcast, or other proof of authority. Google's algorithm now reads this carefully.

4. Optimize for Search Intent, Not Just Keywords

If someone searches "best coaching certification," they want reviews and comparisons, not a tutorial. If someone searches "how to become a coach," they want a step-by-step guide. Write to the intent, not the keyword. Use tools like Semrush or Ahrefs to see what's currently ranking and analyze the intent.

5. Build High-Quality Backlinks from Relevant Domains

A single backlink from Forbes or Business Insider is worth 50 links from random blogs. Focus on getting links from authoritative sites in your industry. Tactics: guest posting on industry publications, having podcast appearances (which often get transcribed with links), and creating linkable assets like frameworks or tools.

Expected Timeline

SEO is the slowest of the 4 pillars. New websites typically see first results in 3-6 months, meaningful rankings in 6-12 months. But it compounds, in year 2, you'll rank for hundreds of keywords and get consistent traffic.

What Is AEO?

What It Is

AEO is optimizing for Google's Featured Snippets and the new AI Overview results. Instead of getting a click on a blue link, your content appears as the answer in the search results themselves.

How Featured Snippets and AI Overviews Work

When Google displays an AI Overview or Featured Snippet, it extracts a direct answer from a webpage. This happens in two places:

  • Featured Snippets (Position 0): A box that appears above the organic results showing the direct answer to a question
  • AI Overviews: A box that appears above the organic results with a multi-source summary generated by AI

Here's the critical insight: you don't have to rank #1 to appear in a Featured Snippet or AI Overview. You just have to have the best-formatted answer to a question.

5 Must-Do AEO Tactics for 2026

1. Use FAQ Schema Markup on Your Pages

Wrap your FAQs in JSON-LD FAQ schema. Google indexes this and uses it for Featured Snippets. Format: <script type="application/ld+json">{"@type":"FAQPage","mainEntity":[{"@type":"Question","name":"...","acceptedAnswer":{"@type":"Answer","text":"..."}}]}</script>. Every blog post should have 5-10 FAQs with schema markup.

2. Write Direct, Concise Answers (60-100 Words)

Featured Snippets favor direct answers. After your intro paragraph, add a bold sentence that answers the question directly, followed by a 60-100 word explanation. For example: "The best time to launch a coaching business is Q1 or Q4. These quarters have highest intent from people making New Year's resolutions or year-end career changes. We recommend..." Keep it concise and directly answer the question.

3. Use Numbered Lists and Tables

Google's Featured Snippets show numbered lists and tables. If you want to rank for "steps to become a coach," format your answer as a numbered list. If you want to rank for "coaching certification comparison," use a table. Match the format to the question intent.

4. Add Definition Boxes for Key Terms

For any technical term or industry jargon, add a definition box early in your article. Format: "Term: A clear, 1-2 sentence definition." Google's AI will pull this for Featured Snippets when someone searches for definitions.

5. Create Question-Based Content

Write articles that directly answer common questions. Use tools like SEMrush, Ahrefs, or Google Search Console to find questions people are asking. Create one article per question, with the question as the H2, then the answer. This naturally creates Featured Snippet opportunities.

Expected Timeline

AEO has much faster results than SEO. If your content is already ranking, adding AEO markup takes 1-2 weeks. You can see Featured Snippet placement within 2-4 weeks. It's the highest-ROI optimization.

What Is GEO?

What It Is

GEO is optimizing for Google's AI-generated results (the AI Overview that appears above organic links). It's different from AEO because the AI is generating a summary from multiple sources, not pulling a Featured Snippet.

How Google's AI Overview Works

Google's AI reads your content and decides: "Is this a trusted, expert source on this topic? Should I cite this?" This is radically different from traditional SEO ranking.

The algorithm considers:

  • E-E-A-T: Does the author show experience, expertise, authority, and trustworthiness?
  • Original insights: Does this page have unique data or perspective?
  • Topical authority: Are you the go-to expert on this topic?
  • Entity clarity: Does your brand have a clear Wikipedia page, structured data, and brand signals?

5 Must-Do GEO Tactics for 2026

1. Build Your Entity (Wikipedia, Wikidata, Google Knowledge Panel)

Google's AI references your entity across the web. Create a Wikipedia page if you're a person of note. Ensure your name is in Wikidata. Get structured data on your website (Schema.org). Google's Knowledge Panel pulls from these sources. A strong Knowledge Panel increases your credibility in AI Overviews.

2. Write in First-Person, Show Your Experience

Google's AI loves first-person writing that shows experience. Instead of "Most coaches struggle with pricing," write "In our 500+ coaching clients, we've noticed that pricing is the #1 constraint. Here's what we found..." Use your own data, case studies, and experience. This differentiates you from AI-generated content.

3. Get Media Mentions and Backlinks from Authoritative Sites

When Forbes, HubSpot, or industry publications mention you, Google's AI sees you as authoritative. These mentions are factored into AI Overview selection. Focus on getting quoted in industry publications, being featured in podcasts that are transcribed, and getting mentioned in research reports.

4. Use Structured Data (Schema.org) Extensively

Add Schema.org markup for: Person, Organization, Article, BlogPosting, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList. Google's AI reads this structured data to understand your expertise and authority. Tools like Schema.org validator can help you audit your markup.

5. Create Topical Authority Clusters

Don't write about 50 different topics. Focus on 3-5 core topics and become the best resource on each. For example: "Coaching Business," "Lead Generation," and "Pricing Strategy." Google's AI recognizes you as the authority on these topics across all your content.

Expected Timeline

GEO results come in 2-3 months if you already have authority. If you're starting from scratch, it takes 6-12 months to build enough authority for AI Overview inclusion.

What Is LLMO?

What It Is

LLMO is optimizing for visibility in LLMs like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and other AI assistants. These systems use different sources than Google and have different ranking factors.

How LLMs Decide What to Recommend

LLMs use several sources:

  • Training data: Public web content, Reddit, Medium, Twitter (data they trained on)
  • Web search results: When they do web searches to answer current questions
  • Structured data: Knowledge bases, databases, and llms.txt files
  • Direct integrations: Some LLMs have direct integrations (e.g., ChatGPT's browsing)

The key insight: LLMs don't rank websites. They reference sources. If your content is high-quality and appears in the sources they use, it gets recommended.

5 Must-Do LLMO Tactics for 2026

1. Build a Strong Reddit Presence

LLMs training data heavily includes Reddit. When someone searches for coaching advice on Reddit, if you're giving expert answers (and it's clear you're an expert), LLMs will reference your comments. Start answering questions in r/coaching, r/businesscoaching, r/entrepreneurs. Link to your best blog posts in your answers. Add a Reddit flair showing you're an expert.

2. Write on Medium or LinkedIn (Places LLMs Index)

LLMs index Medium and LinkedIn heavily. Write 1-2 pieces per month on Medium or LinkedIn about your expertise. These get indexed by LLMs and increase your visibility when someone asks about your topic. Make sure your content is original, expert-level, and links back to your website.

3. Get Listed in Directories and Databases

LLMs reference directories. Get listed in: industry directories (Coaches Federation, if applicable), professional listings (LinkedIn, AngelList if relevant), and any database relevant to your field. When an LLM searches for a recommendation, it often cites directory listings.

4. Create an llms.txt File on Your Website

This is new (2025). You can create a public llms.txt file that tells LLMs about your business, services, and key facts. File location: yoursite.com/llms.txt. Format: key-value pairs. LLMs read this when they index your site. This ensures they have accurate information about you.

5. Optimize for Podcast Discovery

Many LLMs index podcast transcripts (Spotify, Apple Podcasts). If you're a podcast guest, ensure your name, company, and credentials are clearly mentioned in the show notes and transcript. Start your own podcast and transcribe episodes. This creates multiple touchpoints for LLM discovery.

Expected Timeline

LLMO is the fastest. Reddit visibility: 1-2 weeks. Medium/LinkedIn articles: 2-4 weeks. Directory listings: 1-2 weeks. Podcast: 2-8 weeks depending on distribution.

How the 4 Pillars Interact and Reinforce Each Other

The genius of the 4-pillar approach is that they reinforce each other. Here's how:

Strong SEO: When you rank #1 on Google, that ranking signal increases your authority (helps GEO). Your top-ranking content gets more backlinks and citations, which are used in LLM training data (helps LLMO).
Strong AEO: Featured Snippets and AI Overview placements increase click-through rate, which improves SEO rankings. The visibility also drives brand awareness, which helps GEO (more mentions, authority).
Strong GEO: Building authority through media mentions, backlinks, and E-E-A-T helps SEO rankings and LLMO credibility. Your brand knowledge panel becomes stronger, which helps all 4.
Strong LLMO: When LLMs recommend you, that drives brand awareness and traffic. Brand queries increase (helping SEO). Authority increases (helping GEO). You get mentioned in LLM conversations (helping authority overall).

The compound effect of all 4 is exponential. A company optimizing for all 4 pillars gets roughly 2-3x more visibility than a company optimizing for only SEO.

How Does the ELEVATE Framework Work?

You can't do everything at once. Here's the recommended sequence using the ELEVATE Method:

Months 1-2: Foundation (SEO + AEO)

  • Audit your current content for quick AEO wins (add FAQ schema, create answer boxes)
  • Start creating pillar pages and topic clusters (SEO foundation)
  • This gives you quick wins (AEO) while building long-term foundation (SEO)

Months 2-4: Authority (GEO)

  • Add author bios and E-E-A-T signals to content
  • Start PR outreach (get mentioned in publications)
  • Create original research or case studies
  • Build your brand entity (Wikipedia, Knowledge Panel)

Months 4+: Community (LLMO)

  • Start answering on Reddit, Twitter, relevant forums
  • Write on Medium or LinkedIn monthly
  • Get listed in directories
  • Create llms.txt file

Months 6+: Compounding

  • By month 6, all 4 pillars are working together
  • SEO results start appearing (month 6-12)
  • Authority from GEO helps all channels
  • Community from LLMO drives consistent traffic

What Does a 90-Day Implementation Plan Look Like?

Week 1-2: Audit

  • List your top 50 keywords and check: Are you ranking? Are you in Featured Snippets? Are you in AI Overviews?
  • List your top 20 pieces of content and audit them for AEO (FAQ schema, direct answers, formatting)
  • Check: Do you have author bios? E-E-A-T signals? Knowledge Panel?

Week 3-4: Quick Wins (AEO)

  • Add FAQ schema to 20 blog posts (1-2 hours each)
  • Add direct answer boxes to 20 pieces (30 min each)
  • Reformat lists and tables for Featured Snippet optimization (30 min each)
  • Expected result: 3-5 new Featured Snippets in weeks 3-4

Week 5-8: SEO Foundation

  • Create 3 pillar pages (3,000+ words each)
  • Create 9 child pages (1,500+ words each) that link to pillars
  • Internal linking: 3+ links from child pages to pillar, 2+ links between child pages
  • Expected result: Foundation for 6-12 month compound SEO growth

Week 9-12: Authority & Community

  • Add author bios and E-E-A-T to all pillar pages
  • Reach out to 20 journalists/publications for PR
  • Start answering on Reddit (1-2 hours/week)
  • Write 1 Medium or LinkedIn article
  • Expected result: First media mentions, Reddit authority, LLMO visibility

What Not to Do: Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Thinking SEO alone is enough. If you're only optimizing for Google's blue links, you're invisible in 47% of searches (AI Overviews) and to 35% of Gen-Z. Start with SEO but layer in the other 3 pillars.

Mistake 2: Ignoring AI Overview optimization (GEO). Most businesses have done nothing to optimize for Google's AI. This is a huge opportunity. Add E-E-A-T signals, build authority, and get media mentions. AI Overview visibility is low-hanging fruit.

Mistake 3: Writing generic content optimized for keywords, not humans. LLMs and Google's AI both prefer authentic, expert content. Stop writing for algorithms. Write for humans who know your field.

Mistake 4: Ignoring LLMO entirely. LLM search is growing 3x faster than Google search for certain demographics. Get on Reddit, Medium, and in LLM training data. This costs almost nothing and pays dividends.

Mistake 5: Not measuring what's working. Set up tracking for: SEO rankings, Featured Snippet placements, AI Overview mentions, LLM recommendations. What gets measured gets optimized.

What's the Bottom Line?

Search has changed. The companies winning in 2026 aren't the ones with the best SEO. They're the ones visible in all 4 channels: SEO, AEO, GEO, and LLMO.

The good news: You don't need to do everything at once. Start with AEO (quick wins), layer in SEO (long-term), add GEO (authority), and finish with LLMO (community).

By month 6, you'll be visible on Google's blue links, in Featured Snippets, in AI Overviews, and in LLM recommendations. Your competitors will be visible in maybe 1 of these 4 channels.

That's how you win.

FAQ

Do I need to optimize for all 4 pillars, or can I focus on one?

You should build all 4, but in sequence. Start with AEO (quick wins). By month 2, layer in SEO. By month 4, add GEO. By month 6, add LLMO. The compound effect of all 4 is 2-3x more visibility than optimizing for just one.

How much does it cost to implement the 4-pillar approach?

Minimal. AEO is free (just markup). SEO requires either your time or a $500-2,000/month agency. GEO requires your expertise + occasional PR (free or $100-500/month). LLMO is mostly free (time on Reddit, Medium, etc.). Total: $500-2,000/month if you hire, $0 if you DIY.

Which pillar has the fastest ROI?

AEO by far. You can add FAQ schema and get Featured Snippet placements in 2-4 weeks. LLMO is second (1-2 weeks for Reddit visibility). SEO and GEO take longer but compound over time.

What if my competition is already optimizing for all 4 pillars?

Start anyway. Even if they're ahead, you can catch up in 6-12 months by executing better. Most businesses are terrible at execution. Consistent, daily effort beats competitors' hype 90% of the time.

How do I measure success with the 4-pillar approach?

Track: (1) SEO rankings (Semrush, GSC), (2) Featured Snippet placements (SERP monitor), (3) AI Overview mentions (Google Search), (4) LLM recommendations (ask ChatGPT, Perplexity). Set baseline metrics month 1, track monthly, and watch the growth compound.
