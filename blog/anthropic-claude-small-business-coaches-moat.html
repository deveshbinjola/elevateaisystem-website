You started showing up online with the AI angle.

You spent weekends learning Claude.

You bought the $497 course.

You finally figured out how to make an agent draft your follow-ups, summarize your discovery calls, plan your week.

You started telling clients, "I'm the AI guy in this niche."

That sentence still felt rare.

It worked.

Yesterday, it became table stakes.

The Math On Your Old Moat

Here's the uncomfortable part.

The thing you've been selling for the last twelve months... is the thing Anthropic just handed out for free.

You sold "I know how to set up AI for your business."

Anthropic just pre-built 15 of them.

You sold "I'll teach you AI fluency."

Anthropic is doing free half-day workshops with a one-month Max subscription as the closer.

You sold "I'll integrate AI into your tools."

The 7 integrations they shipped this week cover the exact stack most of your clients are already paying for.

The math is simple.

If your competitive edge was "I know AI better than my niche knows AI"... that edge just got commoditized.

Not by another coach. By the company that makes the AI.

The Coach Who Survives This

Stop selling AI.

Start selling the work that the AI cannot do for them.

Look at the compare.

The Moat That Just Caught Fire

  • "I'll set up AI for your business"
  • "I'll teach you AI fluency"
  • "I'll write your prompts"
  • "I'll integrate your tools"
  • "I'll make a custom GPT for you"

The Moat That Survives

  • "I'll find the offer your business is actually selling"
  • "I'll get your voice into every output the AI makes"
  • "I'll rebuild your back-end so agents have something to do"
  • "I'll do the inner work the agents can't"
  • "I'll hold you accountable when the tools forget to"

The pattern is consistent.

The tool layer is racing to the floor.

The strategy, voice, embodiment, and accountability layer is racing to the ceiling.

The coach who survives the next 12 months is not the coach who knows the most tools. It's the coach who is irreplaceable on the parts of the work the tools can't touch.

Why Anthropic Did This Now

This is not a software update.

This is Anthropic figuring out that the enterprise game is too crowded, and the long tail of small businesses is where the money is now hiding.

That long tail is your client list.

The coach making $20K, $40K, $80K a month. The yoga studio doing $50K a month. The consultant doing $300K a year. The agency doing $1M.

All of them are about to get directly served by Anthropic.

Some of them will sign up after this Chicago workshop.

Most of them will not. They will still need someone to translate. Someone to hold the strategy. Someone to design what the agent should actually do.

That's still you.

But only if you reposition before they figure out they don't need you for the install.

What This Looks Like For You This Week

Open the page where you describe what you do.

Read it like you've never met yourself.

If 60% of what you offer can be cloned by a free workshop in Chicago plus a one-month Max subscription, your page needs a rewrite.

Not next quarter. This week.

Here's the rewrite move.

Take every "I'll set up X for you" line.

Replace it with "I'll find the X that's actually worth setting up for your specific business."

The first sentence is a feature. The second sentence is a judgment call.

Anthropic ships features. They don't ship judgment.

Judgment is what people pay coaches for.

Your Move

Don't panic. Don't post about it. Don't comment "this is huge" on a LinkedIn thread.

Do one thing.

Open a blank doc. Write down every offer you sell in 2026.

Next to each one, write the answer to this question:

"If my client walked out of an Anthropic workshop in Chicago last Saturday, would they still need this from me?"

The offers where the answer is yes... double down on those.

The offers where the answer is no... rewrite them or kill them.

That's how you survive a week where the company that makes the tool moves directly into your distribution.

The AI didn't replace coaches.

The AI maker did most of the easy coaching work for you. So the only thing left to sell is the work you were avoiding anyway.

The hard work.

The work the agents can't do.

The work you were originally meant to be doing.
