The Third Path: Most Coaches Grind, Hide, or Burn Out.

The Signal: Almost every coach defaults to one of two states. Grind, which burns out by month eighteen. Or hide, which ships nothing and calls it "still researching." There is a third option almost nobody teaches, and it is the only one that builds a company that lasts.

I watched two coaches collapse this quarter.

One was launching every fourteen days. New offer, new funnel, new lead magnet. He posted at six in the morning and again at eleven at night. He hit a hundred and forty thousand in revenue and then his back went out, his marriage went sideways, and he closed the laptop for six weeks. He called it "a season." It was a nervous system finally cashing the check it had been writing for two years.

The other had not shipped anything in eight months. He kept buying courses. Kept rewriting the avatar doc. Kept "getting the offer right" before he opened the cart. From the outside it looked like research. From the inside it was avoidance with a productive accent. He had four thousand dollars in revenue across the entire year.

Two different coaches. Two different stories. Same underlying mechanism.

They were running their businesses from two of the three possible nervous system states. Neither of them was the one that actually builds a company.

The Two Defaults Almost Every Coach Lives In

Stephen Porges, the neuroscientist who mapped the autonomic nervous system, named three states. Most coaches think these are moods. They are not. They are operating systems. Every decision you make about your business, your offer, your launch, your follow up, your pricing, runs through one of them.

The two defaults are easy to spot once you know what you are looking for.

The first is grind. Sympathetic activation. The body is braced. Heart rate up. Breath shallow. The story in the head says, "If I just push harder, the business will move." The week looks productive from the outside. Inside, the body is paying a tax. The grind coach reaches a ceiling around month eighteen and either burns out, gets sick, blows up a relationship, or quietly stops caring. The revenue chart looks like a heartbeat that finally flatlines.

The second is hide. Dorsal vagal shutdown. The body is heavy. Brain fog. The story in the head says, "I just need a little more information before I am ready." The hide coach buys the course, signs up for the cohort, rewrites the doc, redesigns the website, redoes the avatar work. None of it ships. Hide looks like learning. It is collapse dressed up as study.

Most coaches oscillate between the two. They hide for three weeks, panic about money, switch to grind, push through one launch, exhaust, and slide back into hide. The pattern repeats for years. The business never compounds because the nervous system never lets it.

The Third State Almost Nobody Teaches

The third state is stay. Ventral vagal regulation. The body is settled. Heart rate steady. Breath low. Face soft. Curious. The story in the head says, "Something is moving in the work, and I am going to be present to it." This is the state from which coaches actually build companies that last.

David Deida pointed at this same architecture in a different language. He wrote that most men live inside a binary. Suppress the desire or chase the desire. Puritan or consumer. Repress or indulge. Both are escape from the body. The third path he pointed to is conscious presence through the charge. Stay open. Stay in the body. Stay with the feeling. Do not collapse the tension into either avoidance or release.

Applied to a coaching business, the translation is direct. Most coaches either grind through the discomfort of building (sympathetic, "make the launch save me") or avoid the discomfort entirely (dorsal, "I am still researching"). The third path is to feel the discomfort, stay in the body, and ship anyway. Calmly. Without the urgency that creates desperate offers. Without the avoidance that creates no offers at all.

The reason this state is rarely taught is that it cannot be sold as a tactic. You cannot bottle "stay" and ship it as a 30 day program. You can only practice it. Most coaches do not want to hear that. They want a funnel.

Why Grind Feels Like the Right Answer (And Why That's the Trap)

Most coaches reading this will recognize themselves in grind. Grind feels productive. Grind feels like the thing the coaching influencers told you to do. Grind has a TikTok aesthetic. Grind looks like winning.

It is not winning. It is just motion.

Motion is not the same as alignment. The grind coach is not building a company. He is staving off a panic attack with KPIs. The launches are not strategy. They are sympathetic discharge dressed up as marketing.

You can tell which one you are running by reading your own copy out loud. If the copy sounds like you are out of breath, you wrote it from grind. If the copy sounds like you are reading from inside a fog, you wrote it from hide. If the copy sounds like you are sitting across the table from one person you actually care about, you wrote it from stay.

What Each Path Produces in Year Three

The crisis of the modern coach is that the three paths feel equivalent on day one. On day one, grind and stay both look like "I am building." Hide looks like "I am preparing." Most coaches cannot tell which state they are in because all three feel like progress in week one.

The difference shows up in year three.

What Each Path Builds Over 36 Months
Grind
$0 to peak around $250K, then a cliff. Body breaks. Marriage strains. Six week silence. Comes back smaller and angrier. Quietly closes the business or rebrands it as "consulting" by year four.
Hide
Flatline under $20K, every year. Plenty of energy spent. Plenty of courses bought. No offer shipped that the market could respond to. The notebook is full. The bank account is not.
Stay
$80K, then $180K, then $400K, then a real company. One offer held for years. Compounding referrals. Body intact. Relationships intact. Boring on the timeline, beautiful in the bank account.

The Three Disciplines of the Third Path

Stay is not passive. It is not "chill" or "manifesting" or "trust the process." It is a discipline. Three of them, actually.

One. Stay with one offer for a full quarter. Not three offers. Not a new lead magnet every two weeks. One offer. The coach who switches offers every fourteen days is in sympathetic, even if the offers are well written. Pick one. Hold it for ninety days. The market needs that long to respond.

Two. Notice the state before you make the decision. Before you price an offer, run a ninety second body scan. Feet on the floor. Breath in the belly, not the chest. Jaw soft. Hands warm. If you cannot get to four of those four, do not make the decision. Walk first. The decision will still be there in twenty minutes, and it will be a better decision.

Three. Refuse the launch that comes from panic. If you are launching because rent is due, the launch will read as desperate even if the copy is clean. The reader cannot articulate why, but they will not buy. Skip that launch. Borrow the money another way. Then ship the launch in two weeks from a settled body. The conversion rate difference is not 10 percent. It is often 4x.

The Stay Check
Run this for 90 seconds before any business decision over $1K.
  1. State. Right now, am I in grind (braced, hot face, jaw tight) or hide (heavy, foggy, scrolling) or stay (settled, breath low, curious). Be honest. The body knows.
  2. Breath. Three slow ones. In through the nose for four. Out through the mouth for six. The longer exhale is what shifts the state.
  3. Feet. Press both feet down into the floor. Not metaphorically. Actually press. Notice the support.
  4. Question. If I were settled, would I still want this. If yes, proceed. If no, the urgency was the driver, not the decision.
  5. Ship. If yes, ship. If no, walk for twenty minutes and recheck. Do not split the difference.

The Real Reason Coaches Avoid the Third Path

Stay is uncomfortable in a way grind and hide are not.

Grind has the dopamine of motion. Hide has the comfort of avoidance. Stay has neither. Stay asks you to be present to the actual sensation of building a business, which includes fear, doubt, the slow speed of compounding, the absence of guarantees, and the loneliness of one good decision made quietly when nobody is watching.

Most coaches choose grind or hide not because those paths work, but because the third path requires sitting with a feeling they have been avoiding for years. That feeling is the actual gate. The funnel is not the gate. The avatar is not the gate. The body is the gate.

The coaches who build companies that last are the ones who walked through that gate. They are not louder than the grinders. They are not smarter than the hiders. They are just willing to feel what the other two are running from.

The Real Question

Of the last three big business decisions you made, which state were you in.

If you launched, were you launching because the offer was ready or because you were scared of the bank balance.

If you held back, were you holding back because the work needed more time or because you were avoiding the discomfort of shipping.

If you cannot answer those two questions cleanly, the answer is the answer.

You can rewrite a funnel in a weekend. You can swap a tech stack in a week. You can change a price in five minutes. The state you build from is the only one that takes years to shift, and it is the only one that decides what the business looks like at year three.

Fix the state. The launches will fix themselves.
