Watch yourself on the next discovery call.

Someone shows up stuck.

And you, being generous, hand them the good stuff.

The framework.

The exact sequence.

The thing that took you nine years to learn.

They nod. They thank you. They feel better.

And then they go home and never book.

Most coaches think that's a closing problem.

It isn't.

Here's the uncomfortable truth.

When you give away your framework on a free call or a free post, you didn't build trust.

You removed the reason to pay.

That used to be fine. Implementation was hard. People needed you to walk them through it.

Not anymore.

Now they paste your framework into ChatGPT and the model fills every gap you used to fill.

This week made it louder.

At I/O, Google pulled Canva, CapCut and Adobe straight inside the Gemini app.

The tools you teach are becoming buttons.

Information: free.

Tools: free, and built into the chat box.

The "I know the thing and you don't" advantage is gone.

So here's what the sharper coaches are doing.

They stopped sending people to generic ChatGPT.

Because that hands your audience a brilliant generalist that sounds like everyone and belongs to nobody.

Instead they're building an agent that holds their method.

Their voice.

Their way of seeing the problem.

Not a course. Not a PDF.

A living thing the client talks to that answers the way they would.

The free version qualifies.

The paid version earns.

And the human in the room does the work no machine can reach.

A course teaches information ChatGPT now gives away.

An agent that thinks in your voice gives away nothing.

It becomes the reason someone chooses you in a sea of identical advice.

One is a commodity.

The other is a moat.

Your move:

Open the last three free things you gave away this month.

Ask one question.

Could someone get this exact answer from free ChatGPT in ten minutes?

If yes, you didn't build trust.

You trained a non-buyer.

Now go build the one thing only you can.
