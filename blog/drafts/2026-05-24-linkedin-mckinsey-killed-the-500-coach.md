McKinsey just made the $500 coach free.

Let that sit for a second.

The firm that charges clients millions built an AI tool that does the job of a $200 to $500 an hour interview coach.

Released it free.

10,000 people used it in the first month.

Most coaches read that and feel the floor tilt.

Good.

But don't read it as a threat.

Read it as a map.

Because McKinsey did something else in the same breath that nobody's talking about.

They started putting their own AI inside the final-round interview.

And they're not grading candidates on whether they know the answer.

They're grading them on how they direct the machine.

How they prompt it.

How they read what it hands back.

Whether they catch the moment it's confidently wrong.

The skill that gets you hired at the most selective firm on earth just changed.

It used to be: do you know the answer.

Now it's: can you tell a good answer from a plausible one.

This is bigger than consulting.

For years the expert sold the answer. The framework. The thing you knew that they didn't.

That's free now. Any model gives it away in nine seconds.

But the model can't tell you if that answer is right for you.

It hands the same advice to the burned-out founder and the bored one.

It can't feel the difference.

You can.

That feel has a name. Discernment.

And it lives lower than your head. You know an answer is wrong in your gut before you can argue why.

That signal doesn't come from training data.

It comes from your scars. Your nine years of getting it wrong.

That's the layer a coach owns.

The one no tool has reached.

So here's the move most coaches are about to get wrong:

They'll feel the floor tilt and respond by piling up more information.

More frameworks. More "here's everything I know."

Stocking up on the exact thing that just went to zero.

The ones who win do the opposite.

They stop selling what they know.

They start selling how they see.

Your move:

Pull up your offer. Read it with one question.

Am I selling the answer, or the judgment?

If most of it is "I'll teach you the system, the steps, the framework"...

you're selling the layer McKinsey just made free.

Rewrite it around the part no model can copy.

Not what you know.

How you see.
