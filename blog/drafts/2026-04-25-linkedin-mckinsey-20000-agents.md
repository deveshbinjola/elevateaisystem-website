McKinsey runs 20,000 AI agents.

Alongside 40,000 humans.

18 months ago, it was 3,000.

That's not gradual adoption.

That's a structural shift.

...

And here's the part nobody in the coaching world is talking about.

They didn't slow down hiring.

They're adding 12% more graduates in 2026.

But final-round interviews now include something new.

A live AI collaboration test.

Candidates must use Lilli, McKinsey's internal AI, on a real client scenario.

Then exercise judgment on what it produced.

...

The evaluation isn't "can you use AI."

It's: "do you know when it's wrong?"

...

Most coaches think AI fluency is knowing the tools.

McKinsey just redefined it as something harder.

The ability to think alongside AI without losing your mind in it.

That's a skill.

And right now, almost nobody is coaching for it.

...

Your clients are walking into rooms where AI output is on the table.

And the question they're being asked, quietly, is this:

What did you do with it?

...

There are three layers of intelligence in the AI era.

Tool fluency. That's the floor. Anyone can learn it.

AI judgment. That's what McKinsey tests. Can you push back on the output? Can you see what it missed? Can you adapt it to the specific human in front of you?

Voice and presence. That's the ceiling. The embodied clarity that AI will never replicate.

...

Most coaching programs are teaching the floor.

The coaches who win the next decade will develop the ceiling.

...

Your move:

Look at the executives you coach right now.

Are they confident when AI output is in the room?

Or anxious?

That anxiety is your signal.

That's where the work is.
