Picture your dream client.

Sitting on the couch.

9 pm.

Tuesday.

They open ChatGPT and type "I keep stalling on my business. I think I need a coach. What should I look for."

For two years, you had a fair shot at that conversation.

Sometimes your name came up.

Sometimes a competitor's.

Sometimes neither.

That window is closing.

This week, OpenAI confirmed three things at once.

ChatGPT ads are rolling out across the United States in the next few weeks.

The early pilot is already at $100 million in advertiser spend.

The same pilot just expanded to the UK, Brazil, Japan, South Korea, and Mexico.

Translation.

Every "find me a coach" prompt is about to have a price tag attached to it.

You sell trust.

A coffee brand can outbid you in an ad auction and lose nothing.

You sell the moment a high earning man with a foggy head and a quiet panic decides "this person can help me."

That moment used to live in three places.

Google.

Social.

Word of mouth.

Look at where it lives now.

Google. Monetized.

Instagram. Monetized.

YouTube. Monetized.

ChatGPT. Tilting this week.

Direct audience. Yours to keep.

The first three layers got monetized over a fifteen year stretch.

The fourth just tilted in a single week.

The only one you actually own is the last one.

And almost no coach is investing in it like they own it.

You can play this two ways.

The bidding coach.

Pays to show up. Buys ad slots as they open. Margin gets thinner each year. Rents attention from someone else's machine.

The owned coach.

Builds a voice nobody else has. Owns the list, the inbox, the room. Discovery happens because they are the answer. Not because they paid to be.

You can rent attention forever.

Or you can become the thing the rented attention points to.

The first is a P&L.

The second is a moat.

Three lines on a notecard.

The question my dream client asks at 9 pm.

The answer only I can give.

The asset nobody can outbid me on.

If those three lines are clear, the ad auction does not scare you.

If they are foggy, the next twelve months are going to feel like running uphill on a treadmill someone else owns.

Pick one.

Start there.
