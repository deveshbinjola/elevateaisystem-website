# LinkedIn Templates for ElevateAI

## LinkedIn Post Formula

LinkedIn posts have a unique constraint: only the first 1-2 lines show before "...see more". This is called the hook. Everything else is hidden. This changes the game.

### The Hook (Line 1-2) — Make it count

This is the ONLY thing most people see. It must create curiosity, tension, or intrigue. It must make them click "See more" before they read another word.

#### Hook Types That Work

**Insight Hook:**
```
I stopped posting content for 30 days. My leads went up.
```

**Contrarian Hook:**
```
Your coaching website is invisible to AI. Here's proof.
```

**Story Hook:**
```
When I built ElevateAI, I made a decision that seemed backwards.
```

**Question Hook:**
```
Why are coaches making $50K+/month using systems that seem like common sense?
```

**Result Hook:**
```
We went from $0 to $83K in client revenue in 62 days. This is what we did.
```

**Pattern Hook:**
```
I see this with every coach I work with. They're all making the same mistake.
```

#### What Makes a Bad Hook
- Generic ("Excited to share my thoughts on marketing!")
- Too prescriptive ("Here are 5 tips for better...")
- Salesy ("Looking to scale your coaching? We can help!")
- Obvious ("Did you know that content is important?")
- No curiosity ("Check out my latest blog post")

#### How to Write a Hook
- Start with something specific, not abstract
- Use "I", not "You should" or "People need to"
- Make it about insight, not sales
- Trigger curiosity (Why would leads go up if you stopped posting?)
- Keep it to 1-2 lines before the fold

---

### The Body (Lines 3-15) — Build credibility

Once they click "See more", you have their full attention. Now deliver.

#### Structure
1. **Personal angle** (1-2 sentences) — connect it to your experience
2. **The insight or story** (3-4 sentences) — build the case with examples
3. **Framework or framework application** (3-4 sentences) — teach something
4. **Close with a question** (1 sentence) — drives comments

#### Formatting Rules (CRITICAL for LinkedIn)
- Use **short paragraphs** (1-2 sentences max)
- **White space between paragraphs** (blank lines are essential)
- LinkedIn is not Medium — walls of text get scrolled past
- Break up ideas with line breaks
- Use bold sparingly (max 2-3 bolds per post)

#### Body Formula Example
```
[HOOK]

When I started ElevateAI, I realized something: most coaches build in a vacuum.

They create great content. But nobody finds it.

[STORY/INSIGHT]
Here's what I see over and over:

A coach spends 10 hours writing a blog post. It gets 50 views.
Another coach spends 2 hours. It gets 5,000 views.

The difference? One has distribution. The other has a blog.

[FRAMEWORK]
This is where the ELEVATE Method comes in.

It's not about creating more content. It's about being found by the right people.

SEO + AEO + GEO + LLMO.

Each pillar does something different.

[CLOSE/QUESTION]
Which of these pillars are you weakest on?
```

#### Things to Include
- **Specific numbers** ("83% of coaches...", "I went from $10K to $50K...")
- **Personal angles** ("I see this with every client I work with...", "When I built ElevateAI...")
- **Frameworks** (ELEVATE Method, Content OS, Lead OS, Growth Engine)
- **One surprising insight** (something that makes them stop scrolling)
- **An ending question** (what did you think?, which one applies to you?)

#### Things to Avoid
- **Wall of text** (use white space)
- **"I'm humbled to announce..."** (tired opener)
- **Generic inspiration** ("If you believe in yourself, you can achieve anything!")
- **Emoji bullets** (just use plain text)
- **Hashtag in the hook** (put them at the bottom only)

### Post Types to Rotate

Don't post the same type every day. Rotate through these:

#### 1. Insight Post (Share a non-obvious insight from the blog)
```
[Hook: The insight stated as a bold claim]

[Body: Story or pattern you see, with example]

[Framework: Connect to ELEVATE Method or another framework]

[Close: Question]
```

Example: "Most coaches think distribution is luck. It's not. It's a system. Here's how I built ours..."

#### 2. Framework Post (Teach one of your frameworks)
```
[Hook: Problem statement or surprising insight]

[Body: Why this problem exists, real example]

[Framework: Introduce the framework, explain each pillar/part]

[Close: Question about which part they need most]
```

Example: "Your content is invisible to AI. Here's why—and how to fix it with the ELEVATE Method..."

#### 3. Result Post (Share a client result or case study)
```
[Hook: The result ($X in revenue, X leads, X days to result)]

[Body: The situation before, what they were doing wrong]

[Framework: What changed (systems, mindset shift, tool)]

[Close: Question about what surprised them most or what outcome they want]
```

Example: "One of our coaches went from $0 to $83K in client revenue in 62 days. Here's exactly what changed..."

#### 4. Contrarian Post (Challenge a common belief)
```
[Hook: The contrarian take (You don't need X, The secret isn't Y)]

[Body: Why everyone believes the wrong thing, pattern you see]

[Insight: What actually works instead]

[Close: Validation question (Have you experienced this?)]
```

Example: "You don't need more followers. You need followers who are ready to hire. Here's the difference..."

#### 5. Story Post (Personal narrative that connects to the blog topic)
```
[Hook: Story setup (When I..., I realized..., I made a decision...)]

[Body: The story, what happened, what you learned]

[Insight: What this means for coaches reading]

[Close: How they can apply it]
```

Example: "When I hired my first AI, I fired my copywriter. Here's what happened—and why..."

## What NOT to do on LinkedIn

### Openers to Avoid
- "I'm humbled to announce..." (tired and insincere)
- "Can we take a moment to..." (always feels manipulative)
- "If you're serious about..." (gate-keepy)
- "Here are 5 tips for..." (too prescriptive, not enough story)
- "I just realized..." (vague setup)

### Formatting Mistakes
- Wall of text (use line breaks)
- Emoji bullets (LinkedIn doesn't need them)
- ALL CAPS sections (feels like yelling)
- Multiple hashtags in the body (put them at the end)
- 10+ hashtags (overkill)

### Sales Mistakes
- Salesy hook ("Book a call with me!")
- Every post is a pitch ("Our new program...", "Limited spots...")
- Urgent language ("Don't miss out!", "Only 3 spots left")
- No value before the ask (give something first)
- Asking for business in the close (ask a question instead)

### Content Mistakes
- Generic motivation (everyone else posts it)
- No personal angle (sounds corporate)
- No specificity (numbers make posts)
- No framework or structure (insights without context)
- Too long (LinkedIn scrolling threshold is lower than you think)

## LinkedIn Engagement Tips

### Drive Comments (Not Just Likes)
- End with a genuine question (not "What do you think?" but "Which of these have you made?")
- Create space for disagreement (people comment more on contrarian posts)
- Share data or a surprising stat (people comment to verify or add)
- Tell a story that ends with a reflection question (people relate and respond)

### Timing
- Post in the morning (6-9 AM in your audience's timezone)
- Post on weekdays (Tuesday-Thursday get more engagement)
- Avoid Sunday/Monday (lower engagement)
- Post when your audience is online (for coaching audience, typically early morning or evening)

### Consistency
- 3-4 posts per week (don't spam, but stay visible)
- Mix post types (don't do framework posts 5 days in a row)
- Engage with your own comments (signal to algorithm)
- Repost top performers at different times of week

### Amplification
- When you get 20+ comments, add a comment of your own thanking people
- When someone tags you or mentions you, respond within hours
- Share others' great posts (not just your own)
- Engage on posts in your niche before posting your own

## Post Length Guide

- **Short posts** (under 300 chars): Good for quick insights, contrarian takes, or hooks that drive engagement
- **Medium posts** (300-800 chars): Sweet spot for story posts and insight posts
- **Long posts** (800-1200 chars): Framework posts, detailed strategies, thought leadership

Don't go over 1200 characters. Beyond that, people stop reading before they get to the question at the end.
