Introduction

What is a Brand OS?

Your Brand OS is four documents: Avatar · Voice · Pillars · Scripts. Together they answer the only question that matters when anyone encounters your marketing: "Is this for me?"

Most coaches don't have a Brand OS. They have fragments. A bio that sounds like everyone else's. A few content ideas that work and a dozen that don't. A pitch that shifts depending on who's asking. The result: people who'd love what you do never realize it, and people who aren't right for you take up your DMs.

A Brand OS fixes this because it forces you to answer four non-negotiable questions in writing. Once you have the answers, everything downstream — content, lead gen, offers, sales — becomes execution, not invention.

The four documents

Avatar — One specific person you serve, described in enough detail that you could pick them out of a room.

Voice — How you sound when you're at your best. The words you use, the rhythm you speak in, the energy you bring.

Pillars — The 3–5 themes you own. The things you could write about for 10 years without running out.

Scripts — The 4 reusable messages you'll use 1,000 times — elevator pitch, cold outreach, discovery call opener, referral ask.

Why this order matters

Avatar comes first because you can't write voice for no one. Voice comes second because you can't pick pillars without knowing how you'll sound in them. Pillars come third because scripts are pillar-flavored. Each document feeds the next.

How to use this workbook

Option 1 — In one sitting (90 min): Best for when you have a quiet morning and enough coffee.

Option 2 — 20 min a day for 4 days: Module per day. Sleep on each one.

Either way: write first, edit later. Don't hunt for the perfect answer on page one. Get something down. You can refine every document over time — but you can't refine nothing.

Module 01 · 25 minutes

Your Avatar

You're going to write about one specific person. Not a demographic. Not a customer segment. One real human with a name, a life, a pain, and a desire. Marketing to "entrepreneurs who want more" is marketing to no one. Marketing to "James, 38, recovering high-performer, two kids, moved from finance to men's coaching after his divorce" is marketing to thousands of exactly-right people.

The rule

If the person reading your content thinks "how did they know that about me?" — you did this right. If they think "I could be this, or I could be that" — you went too broad.

Your Voice

Module 03 · 20 minutes

Your Pillars

Pillars are the 3 to 5 themes you could write about for 10 years without running out. They're the terrain you own. Every piece of content you publish should trace back to one of them. When you don't have pillars, your content reacts to the algorithm. When you do, the algorithm reacts to you.

The pillar test

A real pillar passes three tests: (1) you can write 50 posts about it without reaching, (2) your avatar searches for something inside it, and (3) your offer sits inside it. If a pillar fails any test, cut it.

Module 04 · 25 minutes

Your Scripts

Scripts are the 4 messages you'll reuse 1,000 times. Elevator pitch. Cold outreach. Discovery call opener. Referral ask. Write them once in your Voice, directed at your Avatar, inside your Pillars — and you never think about them again.

The rule

A script is not a template you copy verbatim. It's a skeleton you can riff on. Write the bones so the muscles can flex.

Script 4.1 · Elevator Pitch 6 min

"What do you do?"

The single question you'll answer every week for the rest of your career. Write the version that filters in your people and filters out the wrong ones.

Skeleton

"I help [Avatar] [move from pain to desire] using [your unique mechanism]."

Example: "I help high-performing men who've lost themselves inside their own success rebuild an embodied identity — using an AI-powered daily practice system."

Script 4.2 · Cold Outreach 7 min

First DM to a cold prospect.

3–4 sentences. Specific. Not salesy. Shows you actually looked at them.

Skeleton (4 beats)

1. Specific observation about them (not generic praise).

2. What caught your attention and why it connects to what you do.

3. Offer something small and concrete (not "hop on a call").

4. Exit ramp: "No worries if not."

My cold outreach DM:
Script 4.3 · Discovery Call Opener 6 min

First 90 seconds of a sales call.

Your first words set the frame. If you lead with "how's your week going" you've already lost it.

Skeleton

1. Thank them for the time, name the duration (30 min, 60 min).

2. State the outcome of the call, not the agenda.

3. Ask one surgical opening question — not "tell me about yourself."

Script 4.4 · Referral Ask 6 min

The 2-line message sent at the right moment.

Sent to a client who just had a win, or someone who shared your work. Makes referrals frictionless.

Skeleton

"[Win] — so glad. Quick one: if you know anyone like [very specific avatar descriptor], I'd love an intro. Not urgent."
